# EOG original-idea virtual-world synthesis

## Scientific question

The virtual-world programme was created to test the original EOG idea rather than BAM
combinatorics for their own sake.

The original claim can be stated as:

> An occurrence is evidence not only that a state can exist locally, but that it was
> reached within at least one admissible distribution-forming history.  Therefore
> spatial relations among occurrences, pathwise continuity, source/history ambiguity,
> and claims that survive many plausible worlds can contain information that a static
> cellwise suitability map discards.

BAM supplies useful A/B/M coordinates for constructing controlled ecological worlds.
It is not the sole scientific target.

The programme progressively asked five questions:

1. Are the original EOG distinctions real under known truth?
2. How much information do positive occurrences contain about reachability/history?
3. What happens to robust conclusions when the declared world universe grows?
4. What does a static BAM/raster representation lose about topology?
5. What relational evidence is sufficient for a declared ecological target, and how
   should it be collected adaptively?

## I. Existence, generality and analyst worlds — v1 to v3

### v1: canonical archetypes

The preregistered archetypes separated seven quantities cleanly:

- local viability versus reachability;
- endpoint environmental similarity versus pathwise environmental continuity;
- stepping-stone dependence;
- bottleneck versus redundant routes;
- same final occurrence set with different histories;
- static versus time-stamped history evidence;
- monotone weakening of universal certificates under world expansion.

All seven canonical hypotheses were supported.

This was an existence result only.

### v2: randomized ecological landscapes

The same mechanisms were tested in 384 randomized 7x7 landscapes.

Seven of eight preregistered hypotheses were supported.

Strong general patterns included:

- viable-but-unreachable fraction rose from 0.258 at barrier density 0.05 to 0.583 at
  0.35;
- critical stepping-stone fraction was higher in rook than queen neighbourhoods
  (0.359 versus 0.229);
- queen neighbourhoods were more robust to a single-node loss;
- median static source-history alias count was 21;
- time-stamped positives contracted source-history aliases by median 0.815 among
  eligible rows;
- multiple minimal rescue classes occurred, including barrier-only, environment-only,
  either-axis, jointly-required and non-edge-rescuable cases.

The important refutation was G2.

The preregistered expectation that low environmental autocorrelation would increase
pathwise IBE failures was reversed:

- low autocorrelation blocked fraction: 0.052;
- high autocorrelation: 0.232.

### v3: analyst-world uncertainty

v3 showed why the G2 reversal mattered.

Holding the ecological landscapes fixed while changing only the
response-independent rule translating environmental difference into traversability
made 36.6% of locally permissive target classifications analyst-world contingent.

The autocorrelation contrast changed sign across analyst rules:

- q70 rule: -0.180;
- absolute tolerance 0.5: +0.634;
- standardized 1 SD: +0.213.

But the barrier-density effect stayed positive under all three rules.

Therefore the programme established an important EOG distinction:

> ecological-world uncertainty and analyst/representation-world uncertainty are
> separate objects; some conclusions are analyst-contingent while others survive both.

## II. What occurrence points actually tell us — v4 and v5

### v4: occurrence as reachability evidence

Candidate worlds crossed unknown source, barrier level and analyst rule.

Treating an observed point only as locally viable retained the entire candidate
universe.  Requiring that it also be reachable strongly contracted the world set.

Mean surviving world fractions under clustered anchors were:

- 10% coverage: 0.580;
- 25%: 0.537;
- 50%: 0.502;
- full positive coverage: 0.447.

Dispersed anchors were more informative at equal point counts:

- 25%: 0.470 versus 0.537;
- 50%: 0.456 versus 0.502.

Spatial arrangement changed survivor counts in 211/314 eligible landscapes.

Yet full positive coverage still left:

- median 104 candidate worlds;
- median 30 candidate sources;
- exact world recovery: 0/314;
- exact source recovery: 0/314.

So the original idea was partly confirmed:

> occurrence geometry contains reachability information beyond local viability, but
> even complete static positive occurrence clouds need not identify source or history.

O5 was refuted.

More static positive occurrences reduced world identity without improving the robust
impossibility set.  Robust-impossibility recovery remained 0.394 at every coverage
level under both sampling designs.

This exposed a separation between:

- world/history contraction;
- target-level spatial exclusion.

### v5: direction and temporal order

Adding directed flow changed the information content dramatically.

Full-coverage mean surviving source count fell from:

- undirected static: 27.14;
- directed static: 2.15;
- directed temporal: 2.06.

Exact source recovery increased from:

- 0 rows;
- to 53;
- to 71.

Robust-impossibility recovery also increased strongly when direction was introduced:
from about 0.191 under undirected worlds to 0.655 at full directed coverage.

But D5 was refuted:

> temporal order further contracted source/world identity without increasing
> full-coverage robust-impossibility recovery beyond directed static evidence.

Thus evidence that identifies history is not automatically evidence that changes the
spatial possibility envelope.

## III. World growth, robust certificates and future-proof evidence — v6 to v9

### v6: impossibility was not intrinsically more robust

The original intuition that impossibility should survive broader world universes better
than reachability was tested directly and refuted.

Under the frozen mixed world expansion:

- robust-reachable certificate retention: 96.1%;
- robust-impossible retention: 64.8%.

The direction was reversed in every barrier-density, autocorrelation and neighbourhood
stratum.

So the correct rule is not:

> impossibility is inherently more robust.

### v7: expansion direction supplies the missing condition

Reachable-set inclusion classified added same-source worlds as restrictive,
permissive, equivalent or incomparable.

Exact protection laws held with zero violations:

- restrictive additions protected every baseline robust-impossible certificate;
- permissive additions protected every baseline robust-reachable certificate.

The opposite certificate classes were vulnerable:

- restrictive worlds erased 81 reachability certificates;
- permissive worlds erased 1,445 impossibility certificates.

Incomparable worlds and new sources added further fragility.

The corrected principle is:

> certificate robustness is conditional on the direction in which the declared world
> universe can expand.

### v8: certificate lifetime under progressive world growth

v8 converted the survive/fail endpoint into an exact finite world-growth survival curve.

Threat count exactly determined certificate persistence under uniformly random addition
of the frozen future-world pool.

Under full all-source growth:

- robust-reachable mean normalized expected lifetime: 0.974;
- robust-impossible: 0.715.

Restrictive same-source growth gave impossibility full lifetime.
Permissive same-source growth gave reachability full lifetime.

This turned EOG robustness into a graded quantity indexed by a declared growth pool.

### v9: current diagnosis and future-proofing are different evidence objectives

Three diagnostics were compared:

- source identity;
- barrier state;
- analyst rule.

Source measurement produced strong immediate current-world contraction.
Barrier and analyst-rule diagnostics produced zero current B0 contraction by design.

Yet they could have larger future-proof value.

Across 272 rows:

- current-contraction optimum and future-proof optimum disagreed in 178;
- zero-current-effect actions had positive future value in 205;
- future-proof optimal action was source in 94 rows, barrier in 91, analyst rule in 87;
- one diagnostic immortalized 1,585 previously threatened certificates.

Therefore:

> evidence useful for understanding the current world is not necessarily the evidence
> that best protects a conclusion against future expansion of the world universe.

## IV. What static BAM/raster maps lose — v10 to v13

### v10: identical marginal maps can hide different topology

Four topology worlds were constructed with exactly identical:

- A;
- B;
- M;
- G;
- nodewise marginal score vectors.

Yet pairwise relations, first-passage depths and knockout responses differed.

At 14 active nodes:

- chain versus star relation Hamming distance: 78;
- mean first-passage depth: chain 7.0, branching 2.38, star/redundant 1.0;
- critical-node count: chain 12, branching 6, star/redundant 0;
- knockout retained fraction: chain 0.538, branching 0.893, star/redundant 1.0.

So a static BAM accessibility mask correctly says which nodes are accessible but does
not identify how that accessibility is organized.

### v11: target-specific relational sufficiency

For the four controlled topology worlds, exact minimum relational evidence depended on
the target.

- first passage: 1 measurement;
- intervention response: 1;
- critical-node count: 1;
- pairwise relation: 2;
- joint relational suite: 2.

Full topology identity was unnecessary for narrow targets.

But Q7 was refuted: the combined relational suite identified all four topology worlds
in every row.

Thus target-specific compression has a boundary: a sufficiently rich target bundle can
itself become world-identifying.

### v12: random-topology generality

The static-map/topology information gap generalized to 144 random panels with 12
topology worlds each.

At 12 and 16 active nodes, pairwise-relation and first-passage signatures identified all
12 topology worlds in every replicate despite identical static maps.

Edge richness increased knockout robustness and shortened first-passage depth:

- Spearman edge count versus knockout retained fraction: +0.750;
- edge count versus first-passage depth: -0.405.

### v13: minimal relational evidence in random topologies

The one-measurement shortcuts of v11 did not generalize.

Typical exact target resolution required about three relational measurements:

- pairwise relation mean: 3.264;
- first passage: 3.264;
- intervention: 3.104;
- critical-node count: 2.903.

Nevertheless, all declared targets and even full topology identity remained exactly
resolvable with only a small REL/FP/KO subset and no raw-edge query.

Cross-family sufficiency remained common, and no one relational measurement was
universally first.

## V. Adaptive and cost-aware evidence design — v14 to v16

### v14: outcome-adaptive measurement

Adaptive replanning weakly dominated the best fixed exact panel in every row-target.

Strict worst-case savings occurred in 262 row-target combinations.
Realized-path savings occurred in nearly every row for pairwise relation,
first-passage and intervention targets.

Branch-specific replanning occurred in 131/144 rows.

Narrow ecological targets could stop earlier than full topology identity.

### v15: measurement cost changes the evidence policy

Four frozen synthetic cost worlds altered relative costs of REL, FP and KO.

All targets remained exactly resolvable and adaptive policies retained their advantage.

Changing cost alone changed canonical first action 1,023 times.

Making a family expensive removed that family from canonical first-action choices in
the frozen panel.

Therefore the next measurement is conditional on:

- surviving ecological worlds;
- declared ecological target;
- relative measurement costs.

### v16

v16 asks whether one adaptive policy can remain acceptable when the cost world itself
is unknown.

Its preregistered benchmark has an authoritative completed result and is undergoing the
final current-head reproducibility rerun before integration into this synthesis.

## Strongest surviving conclusions

Across v1-v15, the most defensible original-EOG conclusions are:

1. **Occurrence is not just local viability.**
   Positive occurrence geometry constrains reachability worlds that local support alone
   cannot distinguish.

2. **Static distributions do not identify distribution-forming history.**
   Even complete positive coverage can leave large source/world ambiguity.

3. **Relations and topology are genuine information dimensions.**
   Identical A/B/M/G/marginal maps can imply different first passage, pairwise
   connectivity and intervention response.

4. **Direction and timing matter, but for different targets.**
   Direction can sharpen both source identity and spatial exclusion; time can sharpen
   history identity without changing the spatial envelope.

5. **Robustness is indexed by the declared world universe.**
   Adding worlds can only erode universal certificates, and which certificate class is
   protected depends on expansion direction.

6. **Analyst choices belong inside the uncertainty universe.**
   Environmental conclusions can reverse sign across reasonable response-independent
   traversability rules while barrier conclusions remain robust.

7. **The required information is target-specific.**
   Full mechanism/topology identity is often unnecessary for a narrow ecological
   question, but rich target suites can erase that compression.

8. **Evidence design should be adaptive and cost-aware.**
   No single measurement is universally best; the useful next action depends on the
   surviving world set, target, observed outcomes and cost assumptions.

## Important refutations that changed the theory

The programme is stronger because several intuitive claims failed.

- v2 G2: environmental autocorrelation effect reversed under the frozen q70 rule.
- v4 O5: more static positive occurrences did not increase robust-impossibility
  recovery.
- v5 D5: temporal order improved source/history identity but not full-coverage spatial
  exclusion.
- v6 C4: impossibility certificates were less persistent than reachability certificates
  under the mixed expansion.
- v11 Q7: a rich joint relational target identified complete topology.
- v16 U4: pending final integration, the frozen result found no zero-regret policy shared
  across all cost worlds.

These failures replace broad slogans with conditional rules.

## Current scientific product

The strongest conceptual form of EOG after these virtual-world tests is:

> **A distribution should be represented as a set of admissible, occurrence-conditioned
> distribution-forming worlds with explicit relational structure.  EOG reports which
> node, relation, history or target statements are universal, contingent or excluded
> across that set, and chooses additional evidence according to the declared target and
> uncertainty universe.**

BAM remains useful as an A/B/M world generator and diagnostic coordinate system.
It does not replace relational topology or history.

## What should stop

The programme no longer needs additional synthetic examples merely to show that:

- topology matters;
- positive occurrences contract worlds;
- analyst assumptions matter;
- adaptive evidence design can help.

Those points are already established within the frozen synthetic universes.

After v16 is finalized, further virtual-world work should be allowed only if it tests a
new ecological principle rather than another optimizer or cost scenario.
