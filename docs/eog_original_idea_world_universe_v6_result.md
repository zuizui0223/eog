# Original EOG world-universe certificate persistence — v6 result

## Result

Six of seven preregistered hypotheses were supported.

The central hypothesis was **refuted in the opposite direction**.

> Robust impossibility was not more persistent than robust reachability when the
> declared world universe expanded.

## Frozen world expansion

The same 25% dispersed positive-occurrence evidence was held fixed.

Only the candidate universe changed:

1. U0 — candidate source uncertainty;
2. U1 — add barrier-level uncertainty;
3. U2 — add analyst environmental-rule uncertainty.

Universal certificates could therefore only persist or erode into contingency.

That monotonicity had **0 violations**.

## Direct test of the original impossibility intuition

At U0 there were:

- 2,628 robust-reachable certificates;
- 4,167 robust-impossible certificates.

After full U2 expansion:

### Robust reachability

- retained: 2,525 / 2,628;
- retention: **96.1%**.

### Robust impossibility

- retained: 2,700 / 4,167;
- retention: **64.8%**.

Difference, impossible minus reachable:

**−31.3 percentage points**.

C4 is therefore **REFUTED**.

## The reversal was not confined to one stratum

A secondary diagnostic retained the same direction everywhere.

### By truth barrier density

| barrier | reachable retained | impossible retained |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0.05 | 96.1% | 81.7% |
| 0.20 | 96.4% | 61.9% |
| 0.35 | 95.6% | 49.2% |

### By environmental autocorrelation

- low: reachable 95.4%, impossible 80.5%;
- high: reachable 96.8%, impossible 45.9%.

### By neighbourhood

- rook: reachable 96.4%, impossible 74.0%;
- queen: reachable 96.0%, impossible 56.8%.

Among rows containing both certificate types:

- reachable more persistent: 169;
- impossible more persistent: 26;
- equal: 61.

This diagnostic does not rescue or retest C4. It shows the preregistered refutation was
not produced by one isolated factor cell.

## World expansion did what EOG expected in one important sense

Adding worlds weakened certainty:

- mean contingent fraction U0: **0.0276**;
- U1: **0.1663**;
- U2: **0.2466**.

Barrier expansion erased at least one universal certificate in 152 rows.
Analyst-rule expansion erased additional certificates in 143 rows.

No false universal certificate was created.

## The conceptual correction

The original intuition had two separable parts.

The first survives:

> A claim that remains true across a broader declared world universe is stronger than
> one certified only in a narrow universe.

The second does not survive:

> Impossibility is not automatically the more persistent kind of universal claim.

Whether possibility or impossibility is more fragile depends on what kinds of worlds are
being added.

Adding more permissive worlds preferentially destroys impossibility.
Adding more restrictive worlds can preferentially destroy robust reachability.

Therefore EOG robustness must always be indexed by the **world-universe expansion
direction**.

## Next question

The next valid virtual-world experiment should stop treating world expansion as one
undifferentiated operation.

It should separately expand the universe with:

1. worlds that are strictly more permissive in reachability;
2. worlds that are strictly more restrictive;
3. mixed expansions.

Then test whether certificate asymmetry follows the expansion direction exactly.

That would turn the v6 refutation into a sharper rule about when reverse-impossibility
reasoning is justified.
