## H1 — viability is not reachability

The target was locally viable in both worlds.

- barrier open: reachable;
- barrier closed: unreachable.

So local suitability alone did not determine the realised reachability state.

## H2 — pathwise IBE matters

Source and target had identical endpoint environment:

- endpoint environmental distance = 0.

But the only continuous route crossed an intermediate environmental gap of 10 units.

- endpoint-only rule: reachable;
- pathwise rule: unreachable.

Thus an endpoint environmental comparison can miss an intervening ecological
bottleneck.

## H3 — stepping stones matter

With identical endpoint local viability:

- no stepping stone route: unreachable;
- stepping-stone route: reachable;
- first arrival depth with the stepping stone: 2.

The intermediate node changed reachability without changing the endpoints.

## H4 — bottleneck and redundancy are different

Both worlds could realize the same endpoint occurrences S and T.

- bottleneck world: one simple route;
- redundant world: two independent simple routes.

Knocking out the critical A node:

- bottleneck world: T became unreachable;
- redundant world: T remained reachable.

So final occurrence support does not encode route redundancy.

## H5 — one distribution does not identify one history

The same observed occurrence set {S,T} was compatible with two histories:

- direct history: first arrival at depth 1;
- stepping history: first arrival at depth 2.

Thus complete static endpoint occurrence support need not identify the
distribution-forming history.

## H6 — temporal positives can split static aliases

Before the extra temporal observation:

- compatible histories: fast + slow.

After requiring T to have been reached by t1:

- compatible histories: fast only;
- survivor-fiber contraction: 50%.

Time therefore supplied information that the static final distribution did not.

## H7 — robust impossibility weakens under world expansion

Before adding a permissive world:

- T was robustly unreachable.

After expanding the candidate universe:

- T became possible;
- no node became newly robustly impossible.

This is the intended EOG asymmetry: adding possible worlds can destroy impossibility
certificates, not strengthen them.

## What v1 establishes

The original EOG quantities are distinct and implementable:

- local viability;
- reachability;
- pathwise environmental continuity;
- stepping-stone dependence;
- route redundancy;
- history ambiguity;
- temporal contraction;
- finite-world robust impossibility.

## What v1 does not establish

The archetypes were constructed to expose one mechanism cleanly at a time.

Therefore the next question is not whether more curated examples can be made to work.
It is:

> **Across randomized BAM/reachability landscapes, under what conditions do these
> phenomena appear, disappear, or become unidentifiable from occurrence evidence?**
