**Core distinction:** **what historical differences exist** is not equivalent to **which differences a chosen observation process can recover**.

## 1. One direct, matched, three-source comparison

All **384/384** factorial landscape identities matched. All source-ID checks, union-reachable counts, basin-overlap definitions and equal final-occupancy checks agreed across v18/v19/v21/v22. There were **0** mismatches.

Every design has **three** source populations. This avoids mixing the v18 two-source primary comparison with the v22 three-source history-observation target.

| Within-landscape comparison | Observed result |
|---|---:|
| Dispersed placement has larger reachable fraction | **293/384** |
| Clustered placement has higher worst-source-loss structural retention | **335/384** |
| Both conditions strictly true in the *same* landscape | **281/384** |
| Dispersed placement alone permits exact history ID from transient occupancy snapshots | **139/384** |
| Clustered placement alone permits history ID | **22/384** |
| **Coverage + insurance trade-off, AND dispersed-only history ID** | **130/384** |

The matched average dispersed-minus-clustered reachable fraction was **+0.0670**. The matched clustered-minus-dispersed source-loss retention difference was **+0.3617**.

This is a direct **post hoc joint description within the same synthetic landscapes**. It strengthens the descriptive integration of already frozen v18–v22 results, but it does **not** transform previously separate hypotheses into one preregistered confirmatory experiment.

## 2. The less obvious contrast: strong historical storage can be hard to read

The frozen v21 state was an *idealized equilibrium earliest-source provenance map*. Its cross-history difference remained **greater for clustered source placement in 359/384** landscapes (mean clustered-minus-dispersed difference **+0.6056**).

The frozen v22 observation process did **not** read provenance directly. It was an idealized collection of **complete post-activation binary occupancy snapshots**, at structural times t4, t5, t6, and t8, with source nodes excluded.

Its paired exact-history identifiability table was:

| Complete occupancy-snapshot identification of all three activation histories | Count |
|---|---:|
| Neither source layout identifies history | 14 |
| **Dispersed only** | **139** |
| Clustered only | 22 |
| Both | 209 |

In **131/384** landscapes, *clustered provenance maps differed more across histories* while *only dispersed placement let the complete occupancy-snapshot library recover their activation order*.

### Ecological interpretation, explicitly conditional on the simulated readout

A present ecological state can store a strong historical fingerprint in **one coordinate** (the identity of first-arriving source) without making that history easy to reconstruct from **another coordinate** (presence/absence snapshots).

Concentrated source basins can overlap strongly, producing high historical dependence of provenance. But occupancy-only snapshots can still have overlapping signatures for the three candidate activation histories. Dispersed basins can carry weaker equilibrium provenance differences yet leave more discriminating transient occupation signatures.

**This is a difference between memory *content* and observation *access*, not evidence that concentrated historical effects are intrinsically harder to measure under every assay.** A direct source-label/provenance assay, different time resolution, imperfect detection, or an entirely different set of histories could reverse the ranking. None is evaluated here.

The existing v22 refutation remains in force: among cases where full-history identification was possible, the minimum number of snapshots was **one in both layouts**. This new post hoc analysis concerns the paired **chance that a finite library can identify history**, not a reduced measurement burden conditional on success.

## 4. Literature and claim boundary

Historical contingency and alternative transient states are established ecological ideas:

- [Fukami (2011), Community assembly: alternative stable states or alternative transient states?](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1461-0248.2011.01663.x).
- [Fukami (2015), Historical contingency in community assembly](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-ecolsys-110411-160340).
- [Song et al. (2021), Untangling the complexity of priority effects in multispecies communities](https://doi.org/10.1111/ele.13870).

We therefore do **not** claim to invent ecological memory, source-basin connectivity, network redundancy, or the general idea that temporal observations carry additional information.

The narrower EOG finding is the **matched separation** of three declared structural targets (coverage, worst-source-loss reachability, and historical source-provenance storage) from a **fourth object, observability under a specific temporal occupancy readout**, while every landscape, source set and graph is checked for identity across original frozen experiments.

### What this cannot show

1. **No demographic mechanism**: the removal metric represents loss of reachable nodes under deleted sources, not realized extinction probability, genetic rescue, or fitness.
2. **No universal ecological memory law**: provenance difference and snapshot identifiability are different mathematical observables of a frozen graph/activation process.
3. **No causal mediation proof**: a joint direction pattern is not sufficient to establish that source overlap mediates observability, and the clustered/dispersed placement procedures alter several correlated geometry properties.
4. **No observed natural source-loss validation**: *Lepanthes rupestris* currently has a separate, explicit pre-response qualification STOP; Glanville and NEON EOG programmes already consumed their frozen endpoints and cannot be repurposed as fresh confirmation.
5. **No new third manuscript**: the result is an exploratory bridge between already existing BAM-identifiability and assembly-history papers. It should not create another paper lane without independent, response-qualified ecological evidence.

### v23: the readout itself can reverse which source layout identifies history

The v22 occupancy-only snapshot library was already carried forward unchanged into the **preregistered v23** observation libraries, which additionally include idealized equilibrium earliest-source provenance assays. We have now *post hoc* matched that frozen v23 result to the same 384 three-source landscapes.

- Occupancy-only full-history identification: **clustered 231/384, dispersed 348/384**.
- Provenance-only identification: **clustered 228/384, dispersed 44/384**.
- Combined libraries: **clustered 338/384, dispersed 349/384**.
- **78/384** showed the strict observation-readout crossover: dispersed-only identification from occupancy snapshots but clustered-only identification from provenance assays.
- All 78 crossovers coincided with a greater history-dependent provenance signature under clustering; **75/78** also met the matched coverage–structural-insurance trade-off.

This directly shows that **source geometry is not ranked for historical identifiability without specifying the observation channel**. It does *not* show equal field measurement costs or valid genetic source attribution; the complete identity-revealing assays are idealized.
