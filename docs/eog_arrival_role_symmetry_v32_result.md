## Ecological interpretation

v31 showed that history is stored in different components of present community structure.

v32 now supplies a biological explanation consistent with established priority-effect
theory:

> **whether history is stored in abundance architecture or chiefly in identity depends on
> how interchangeable ecological identities are in the first-arriver role.**

When first-arriver effectiveness varies strongly among identities, as in the microbiome,
history changes the unlabeled abundance architecture itself.

When different identities express a similar first-mover role, as in the grassland,
arrival history mainly determines **who** occupies that role; removing names leaves a much
more similar rank structure.

This connects the EOG storage result to classic ideas about symmetric niche preemption and
species-specific variation in priority-effect strength, without claiming that v32 directly
identifies the underlying interaction mechanism.

## Claim boundary

Do not claim:

- direct discrimination of niche preemption versus niche modification;
- that the entire microbiome system is universally asymmetric or the entire grassland
  system universally symmetric;
- first discovery that priority-effect strength varies among species;
- a universal law from two systems.

Do claim:

> **Across the two completed experiments, the present-state storage channel of assembly
> history tracks how similarly different identities express the ecological role of
> arriving first: highly identity-dependent first-arriver effectiveness produces
> architecture-level memory, whereas a more interchangeable first-mover role produces
> chiefly identity-level memory.**
