# Penobscot real-system BAM bridge v1

## Role

This is a retrospective external bridge from the finite-world BAM programme to a real
dam-removal system.

It is **not** fresh confirmatory evidence. Published aggregate assemblage responses from
the Penobscot restoration programme are already known.

## Why Penobscot

The archive separates the ingredients needed by BAM:

- FishCommunity;
- FishPassage-Upstream and FishPassage-System-wide;
- Geomorphology;
- WaterQuality.

The restoration itself provides a fixed movement intervention sequence: Great Works
Dam removal, Veazie Dam removal, then Howland bypass/fish-passage improvement.

The fish programme sampled before restoration, immediately after restoration and again
roughly eight years later.

## Frozen focal question

The focal species is alewife (*Alosa pseudoharengus*).

The bridge asks whether pre-restoration positive observations leave multiple finite
A/B/M worlds compatible, and whether later positive reaches falsify or contract those
worlds after the connectivity interventions.

Only positive observations are hard ecological witnesses.

## Anti-circularity

A uses all linkable numeric physical/water-quality variables under a response-independent
selection rule.

B uses smallmouth bass only as a finite community-state proxy and retains both
directions of association.

M follows the historical intervention sequence and is not tuned to focal recolonization
speed.

The endpoint is survivor-fiber contraction/falsification, not reproduction of the
published assemblage result.
