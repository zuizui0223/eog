# Cover letter — Ecological Informatics

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“From open data to open inference: a prospective audit of ecological evidence pipelines,”** for publication in *Ecological Informatics*.

Ecological data-sharing standards have made increasingly large and diverse datasets findable and reusable. Yet a public resource may still fail to support a specific ecological claim if the response-independent site registry cannot be reconstructed, response rows cannot be linked to opportunity units, survey zeros cannot be distinguished from missingness, or non-detection has not been calibrated as biological absence.

Our manuscript makes those downstream requirements measurable. We analyze two prospectively governed ecological programmes in which candidate identities, information-access rules and stopping criteria were fixed before focal outcomes. In the first programme, 34 scientific candidate attempts produced three scored predictive endpoints and 31 protocol STOPs. Eleven STOPs occurred at transport/source identity, ten at registry/geometry/time reconstruction, eight at source separation/linkage/schema and two at semantic/covariate validity. Twenty-nine of the 31 STOPs occurred before any biological-response access, and 30/31 occurred before a biological response row value contributed to a scored endpoint.

A second programme tests a later layer of the same problem. Of 12 candidate biological systems, two had independently identifiable relation, event and function source architectures, yet neither had candidate-specific observation-process calibration sufficient to distinguish a true negative function state from non-detection or missingness. No hard biological endpoint was therefore opened.

We call the claim-specific property exposed by these audits **inferential openness**. We position it downstream of FAIR data practice, not as a replacement or additional FAIR principle. FAIR makes resources reusable; inferential openness asks whether a particular data–claim pairing preserves the source, registry, linkage, semantic and calibration states needed to authorize that inference.

We believe this study fits *Ecological Informatics* because it turns normally invisible data-integration and workflow failures into a reproducible ecological-informatics object with a finite denominator. The contribution is both empirical and practical: it identifies concrete information objects that repositories and data papers can preserve to support future claim-ready reuse.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors will approve the submitted version. Code, machine-readable evidence ledgers and frozen workflow records will be archived with a permanent identifier before final submission.
