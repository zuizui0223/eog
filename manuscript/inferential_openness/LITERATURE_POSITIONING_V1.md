# Inferential openness — literature positioning v1

## Position relative to FAIR

The FAIR principles target whether digital resources are Findable, Accessible, Interoperable and Reusable. They explicitly emphasize rich metadata, persistent identifiers, qualified links among data objects, shared vocabularies and sufficient description for reuse.

A 2026 *Ecological Informatics* article by Jantzen & Vriend ("Putting FAIR into practice for ecologists: How to make ecological data more reusable", DOI 10.1016/j.ecoinf.2026.103712) translates FAIR into practical ecological work packages around metadata, storage, standards and structure.

The present paper should **not** claim that FAIR is insufficient in general or propose a replacement FAIR framework.

Instead it asks a different, downstream question:

> Once a public ecological resource is findable/accessibile/reusable enough to enter a scientific workflow, is it **inferentially ready for a particular ecological claim**?

Inferential readiness is claim-specific. It can require information that generic reusability does not by itself guarantee:

- a reproducible spatial/temporal registry for the focal estimand;
- an explicit link between response rows and opportunity units;
- semantics distinguishing surveyed negatives from missing/invalid observations;
- candidate-specific calibration when a non-detection is used as a biological negative.

Thus:

`FAIR/reusable data -> workflow-ready data -> claim-specific inferentially ready evidence`.

The categories are nested conceptually but not equivalent.

## Novelty boundary

Do not write:
- "FAIR data are not reusable";
- "FAIR ignores semantics";
- "we add a fifth FAIR letter";
- "FAIR-compliant datasets fail our audit" unless FAIR compliance was actually measured.

Preferred:
- "FAIR addresses reusable digital resources; our audit measures a downstream claim-specific requirement."
- "A resource may be openly available and reusable for some questions while lacking the registry, negative-state semantics or calibration required for another."
- "Inferential openness is a property of a data–claim pairing, not a permanent score attached to a dataset."

## Related open-science context

Adaptive preregistration work in ecology has recently emphasized registering decision points and preserving transparency across iterative modelling workflows. The EOG/284b evidence adds a complementary empirical object: terminal workflow states generated by those precommitted decisions.

The manuscript should therefore distinguish:

1. **data management / FAIRness** — can the resource be found, understood, accessed and reused?
2. **analytic transparency / preregistration** — were workflow decisions declared and auditable?
3. **inferential readiness** — does the data–claim pair contain the specific states required to support or contradict the endpoint?

The paper's finite STOP denominator exists because (2) was enforced; the substantive object measured is (3).
