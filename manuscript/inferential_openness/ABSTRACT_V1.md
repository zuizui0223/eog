# Inferential openness — abstract v1

Open ecological data are increasingly reusable, but data availability alone does not determine whether a particular biological claim can be evaluated. We audited two prospectively governed ecological inference programmes in which candidate identities, access rules and terminal states were retained rather than repaired after outcomes. In the EOG workflow, 34 scientific candidate attempts yielded three scored predictive endpoints and 31 scientific/protocol STOPs. The STOPs localized to transport or source identity (11/31), registry, geometry or temporal reconstruction (10/31), physical separation, response linkage or schema (8/31), and semantic or covariate validity (2/31). Twenty-nine of 31 STOPs occurred before any biological response access, one after header-only access and one after a single full-response opening, showing that the dominant barrier preceded predictive modelling. A separate 284b screen examined a later inferential layer: 12 candidate biological systems yielded two source-architecture-qualified systems, but neither had candidate-specific calibration sufficient to identify the negative function state required for a hard dependency test; consequently zero focal hard endpoints were opened. These denominators are workflow-specific rather than field-wide prevalence estimates. Together they motivate **inferential openness** as a claim-specific property of a data–analysis pairing: beyond findability and reuse, a claim-ready resource must preserve a reproducible chain of source identity, registry, linkage, observation semantics and calibration. Retaining terminal STOPs makes those usually hidden requirements measurable.
