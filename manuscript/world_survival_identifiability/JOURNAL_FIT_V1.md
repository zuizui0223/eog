# Journal fit audit — world-survival identifiability

Checked: 2026-09-25

## Recommendation

Primary route: Ecological Modelling

Secondary route: Methods in Ecology and Evolution

Fallback route: Ecological Informatics

## 1. Ecological Modelling — strongest fit

Why it fits:
- explicitly publishes new mathematical models and systems analysis for ecological
  processes;
- welcomes ecological theory and conceptual modelling;
- states that applications can refine models and propose new directions;
- the manuscript combines a formal identifiability boundary with a prospective ecological
  validation that rejects a frozen structure-only regime rule.

The paper should be framed as:
- a systems-inference boundary for finite ecological world models;
- a distinction between structural testability and response-conditioned contraction;
- an empirical falsification that motivates the theoretical boundary.

Do not frame it as:
- a new SDM;
- a new machine-learning predictor;
- a software paper;
- a NEON case study with generality asserted from one taxonomic guild.

Primary title style:
Structural testability does not imply predictable contraction in finite ecological world
sets

## 2. Methods in Ecology and Evolution — possible but higher desk-risk

Current MEE scope strongly emphasizes the description and analysis of new methods and
methodological approaches. Research articles normally describe new methods and are
expected to demonstrate broad applicability across taxa or systems. Current guidance also
states that workflows linking existing methods generally are not considered new methods.

Why it could fit:
- the adequacy/survival distinction is a conceptual methodological boundary;
- the EOG finite-world contract is implemented and prospectively validated;
- the response firewall, exact world updating and identifiability audit are reusable.

Why it is riskier:
- the headline result is that a proposed structure-only forecast does not work;
- the graph witness is a proof of a boundary, not a new algorithm;
- a reader could interpret the work as an audit of an EOG workflow rather than a broadly
  new ecological method.

If submitted to MEE, the best article framing would be a conceptual methods paper or
Perspective-style contribution about what can and cannot be certified before response
access in multi-world ecological inference, rather than a new predictive method.

## 3. Ecological Informatics — reasonable computational fallback

The journal explicitly covers computational ecology, ecological data science,
biogeography, modelling, uncertainty analysis and species-distribution modelling.

Why it fits:
- deterministic computational contracts;
- finite-world uncertainty representation;
- model elimination and uncertainty boundaries;
- reproducible NEON implementation.

Why it is weaker than Ecological Modelling:
- the paper's central contribution is inferential and theoretical rather than primarily
  an informatics technique;
- no new data-science algorithm is the endpoint.

## Submission order

1. Ecological Modelling
2. Methods in Ecology and Evolution only if the cover letter foregrounds the broadly
   reusable inferential boundary rather than the NEON result
3. Ecological Informatics

## Desk-screen sentence for Ecological Modelling

This study uses a prospectively frozen finite-world ecological model, a multi-site
empirical falsification and a formal identifiability witness to distinguish what
structural model adequacy can be certified before observations from what can only be
learned after response locations are observed.

## Scope guardrails

The cover letter should say:
- the paper is not proposing a better survival-regime predictor;
- the negative prospective result is part of the contribution;
- the theoretical witness explains why global graph structure alone cannot universally
  determine the later survival state;
- the practical output is a sharper boundary for finite-world ecological modelling.

The cover letter should not say:
- first model to account for dispersal;
- first finite hypothesis-set method;
- first ecological identifiability analysis;
- universal impossibility of forecasting contraction.
