# Cover letter draft — Ecological Modelling

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Structural testability does not imply predictable contraction in finite ecological world sets,”** for publication as an Original Research Article in *Ecological Modelling*.

The manuscript addresses a specific systems-modelling problem: when a finite set of alternative ecological transition worlds is declared before biological response access, which properties of that model set can be certified from structure alone, and which can only be learned after observed occurrences are located within the system?

We separate **structural adequacy** from **evidence-driven world survival**. Structural adequacy asks whether a declared finite world family spans the graph scales required to make a claim testable. World survival asks which of those worlds remain compatible after positive occurrence evidence is observed. We prospectively froze a structure-only rule intended to forecast whether a world set would later be completely falsified, partially contract, or remain saturated, and tested it across 16 fixed sites from the NSF NEON small-mammal programme.

The prospective forecast did not succeed. Nine sites were scorable after once-only response access: seven saturated, two contracted, and none completely falsified, although every site had been forecast to contract. Exact regime agreement was 2/9, and the frozen rule systematically underestimated surviving-world fractions. Importantly, the two intermediate contractions show that the finite-world formulation itself is not restricted to endpoint outcomes.

We then formalize why this failure is more than a poorly chosen threshold. Under the declared one-step positive-evidence rule, world survival is determined by the subgraph induced by observed positive nodes. For any nonempty, noncomplete undirected world graph, the same global graph and the same number of positive nodes can produce either survival or failure depending only on where those positives are placed. Thus structural testability can be audited before response access, but later contraction is not generally identifiable from global graph structure alone without additional assumptions about response location.

We believe this fits *Ecological Modelling* because the paper uses a prospectively falsified ecological model and a formal systems-inference result to refine the boundary of what a finite-world ecological model can support. The negative empirical result is not treated as a failed application to be tuned away; it motivates and is explained by the formal identifiability boundary.

The work is fully reproducible. The repository contains the frozen protocols, response-access audit trail, once-only terminal result, formal implementation and tests, deterministic figure-data files, and a builder for submission figures and supplementary tables. No post-outcome threshold tuning, replacement-site search, or repair of response-consumed registry stops was allowed.

This manuscript is distinct from the separately frozen EOG-WF predictive-complementarity study. The present submission concerns the inferential boundary between response-blind structural testability and response-conditioned world survival; it does not alter or reuse the EOG-WF empirical denominator.
