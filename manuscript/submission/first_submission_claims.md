# Claims for the first Ecological Informatics submission

## Claim 1 — positive A-Islands result

Among held-out islands matched within frozen pointwise-climate-support × nearest-training-source-distance strata, occurrence-conditioned connected frequency retained incidence-ordering information across 845 estimable plant taxa (mean conditional concordance 0.6177466; 95% species-bootstrap interval 0.6086806–0.6269445).

## Claim 2 — decomposition

Geography-only and environmentally constrained connected-frequency summaries both retained positive conditional ordering under the frozen A-Islands scenario definitions. Their descriptive difference is not a predeclared superiority test.

## Claim 3 — strong-reference boundary

In the Tanzania forest-fragment benchmark, adding the generic EOG connected-frequency term after patch area, training-selected matrix-aware current flow, their interaction and nearest training source worsened primary LOSO prediction (+0.0321131 species-macro log loss; 95% interval +0.0174580 to +0.0486750).

## Claim 4 — sensitivity boundary

The Tanzania geometry-only spatial-block sensitivity was smaller and uncertain; it is not evidence that EOG is always harmful or that current flow is universally superior.

## Claim 5 — cross-system conclusion

The incremental value of occurrence-conditioned structural configuration depends on what the declared reference already represents. A structural term can add information beyond local support and direct source distance without necessarily adding value beyond a stronger landscape-specific connectivity model.

## Claim 6 — methodological contribution

The paper contributes a leakage-safe, reference-conditioned validation architecture: explicit estimand separation, outer-training-only occurrence conditioning, predeclared graph-scenario aggregation, matched held-out comparison, explicit failures, a strong-reference falsification benchmark and source-to-manuscript auditability.

## Claims deliberately not made

- first integration of suitability and connectivity;
- first source-conditioned connectivity model;
- first use of occupied-source distance;
- first habitat-network occurrence model;
- first threshold/sensitivity/uncertainty analysis for connectivity;
- new movement probability or colonisation model;
- universal predictive superiority.
