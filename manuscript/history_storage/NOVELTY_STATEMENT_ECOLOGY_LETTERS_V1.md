# Ecology Letters novelty statement — v1

Priority effects are already known to alter community composition, ecosystem processes,
host disease and dominance. The novelty of this manuscript is therefore not the existence
of historical contingency. We ask **where experimentally generated history remains
structurally encoded in the present community**.

The key prospective test removes ecological identity while preserving the exact abundance
multiset, response dimensionality, experimental units, distance metric and history model.
This reveals contrasting storage channels in two independent arrival-order experiments:
microbiome history survives in unlabeled abundance architecture, whereas most grassland
history depends on functional-group identity.

This distinction is not reducible to the familiar composition-versus-function contrast.
It localizes historical contingency within composition itself.

The manuscript also includes a prospective falsification: an initially attractive
first-arriver-role explanation fails complete enumeration of equally complex placebo
mappings. Subsequent frozen tests localize a narrower microbiome legacy to an
Alternaria-associated, host-genotype-shared increase in identity-free community dominance,
while the source archive is explicitly shown to be insufficient for mechanistic
identification.

Relative to the two source publications, the present manuscript asks a different
cross-system question and does not repeat their original biological conclusions. Leopold
& Busby (2020) established host-genotype-dependent effects of fungal arrival order on
microbiome composition and rust disease. Alonso-Crespo et al. (2023) established effects
of plant functional-group arrival order on above- and belowground community structure.
The present work instead decomposes the **structural location of retained assembly
history** across and within those experimentally generated states.
