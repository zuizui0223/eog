# History-storage manuscript — literature positioning v1

## Purpose

This note fixes the manuscript's novelty boundary against the established priority-effects
literature. It is a writing and claim-control document, not a new analysis.

## What is already established

### Arrival order can alter community composition

This is foundational priority-effects knowledge and is not novel here.

Fukami (2015) synthesizes historical contingency caused by the order and timing of
immigration and organizes mechanisms around niche pre-emption and niche modification.

The microbiome literature likewise already treats arrival order as a determinant of
community assembly (Debray et al. 2022).

### Arrival order can alter ecosystem or host function

Also established.

Fukami et al. (2010) experimentally showed that small changes in fungal immigration
history can generate large differences in wood decomposition and carbon release.

Leopold and Busby (2020), the source experiment for the v28 microbiome reanalysis,
already showed that fungal arrival order affects microbiome composition and can alter host
rust disease.

Therefore the manuscript must not present composition-to-function consequences as a new
discovery.

### Assembly-history effects do not obey a universal attenuation law

Dickie et al. (2012) explicitly tested whether assembly-history effects attenuate from
species to community to ecosystem properties and found little evidence for universal
attenuation at the ecosystem level.

The EOG manuscript therefore must not claim that history generally fades as ecological
organization becomes more aggregated.

The supported statement is narrower: in the two reanalysed experiments, retention differs
strongly among particular present-state targets.

### Priority effects can create dominance and evenness differences

Arrival-order effects on community dominance are already documented in plant experiments,
including work connecting early arrival to dominance and productivity.

The v35-v37 contribution is therefore not "priority effects create dominance."

It is the prospective localization of one previously established identity-free
history-storage signal to rank-1 dominance, followed by a direct-history replication and
a host-genotype generality test.

### Grassland arrival history already affects belowground structure

Alonso-Crespo et al. (2023), the source experiment for v30, already showed that plant
functional-group order of arrival modifies vertical root distribution.

The EOG reanalysis does not rediscover that belowground result.

Its contribution is to place the shoot composition and root distribution on the same
frozen history-retention scale and then decompose where the stronger compositional
historical signal resides.

## What the present manuscript adds

### 1. Present-state history retention is treated as a target-specific property

The manuscript asks a different question from whether a priority effect exists:

> Given the same randomized assembly history, which present-state descriptions still
> distinguish those histories?

This is operationalized with matched experimental units and design-respecting
randomization baselines.

The intended novelty is conceptual and comparative, not a claim that partial R² or
permutation tests are new.

### 2. Identity is separated from abundance architecture without changing raw abundances
or dimensionality

The v31 transformation keeps exactly the same abundance multiset and number of response
coordinates, but removes taxon or functional-group identity by sorting abundances.

This prevents the central result from collapsing into the familiar observation that
multivariate composition contains more information than a scalar function.

The two empirical systems then produce contrasting outcomes:

- microbiome history survives identity removal;
- grassland history is much more identity dependent.

This identity-versus-architecture decomposition is the strongest novelty-bearing result.

### 3. A plausible mechanistic story is prospectively falsified

The treatment-indexed first-arriver-role transformation initially produced an attractive
cross-system explanation.

v33 enumerated all equally complex history-to-coordinate mappings and showed that the
biologically correct mapping was not special.

This negative result belongs in the main paper because it demonstrates that the storage
claim does not depend on preserving a post hoc mechanism.

### 4. The microbiome storage signal is localized prospectively

After v33 generated an Alternaria clue, subsequent frozen tests showed that:

- Alternaria had the largest history-level deletion leverage;
- the direct Alternaria-versus-rest contrast replicated the signal;
- leverage was stronger in rank-1 dominance than in the lower-rank tail;
- the dominance shift was positive in 11 of 12 host genotypes;
- most observed binary-history sum of squares belonged to a genotype-shared component.

This is a localization of historical storage, not a discovery that Alternaria or early
arrivals can dominate communities.

### 5. The manuscript explicitly stops before mechanism

Source reconciliation shows that Alternaria had high relative abundance across treatments
but only a relatively modest species-specific early-arrival benefit in the source study.

The archive contains relative amplicon abundance, not absolute fungal load, and lacks the
interaction-level measurements needed to distinguish intrinsic competitive ability,
competitor suppression, niche pre-emption, niche modification, or host-mediated effects.

The mechanistic ceiling is therefore part of the result boundary.

## Claims to avoid

Do not claim:

- priority effects are newly discovered;
- history generally attenuates from composition to function;
- function is less historical than composition in general;
- community dominance as a priority-effect outcome is new;
- the E statistic is a new universal information metric;
- identity-rich and architecture-rich communities are universal classes;
- first-arriver-role symmetry explains the storage contrast;
- Alternaria has the strongest species-specific priority effect;
- Alternaria is necessarily the dominant taxon in each focal plant;
- niche pre-emption, niche modification, direct antagonism, or host mediation;
- generality beyond the two completed experimental systems.

## Strongest defensible manuscript claim

> **Assembly history is not stored in one universal feature of the present. In two
> independent randomized assembly experiments, historical retention differed strongly
> among present-state targets, and removing ecological identity revealed that the
> compositional legacy itself can reside in different structural components: unlabeled
> abundance architecture in one system and predominantly identity-to-abundance assignment
> in another.**

The microbiome follow-up adds a system-specific refinement:

> **Within the microbiome, the largest history-level contribution to identity-free
> architecture is an Alternaria-associated, broadly host-genotype-shared increase in
> rank-1 dominance.**

## Writing rule

Whenever a sentence can be paraphrased as "priority effects do X," assume that the basic
fact is already known and cite it as background.

Reserve novelty language for the localization of **where the historical signal remains
encoded in the present state**, the identity-stripping comparison, and the prospective
falsification/localization chain.
