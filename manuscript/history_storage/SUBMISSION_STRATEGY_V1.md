## What is already established in the literature

The manuscript must not sell any of the following as new:

1. arrival order / priority effects can alter community composition;
2. priority effects can alter ecosystem or host function;
3. history effects do not necessarily attenuate monotonically from species to community
   to ecosystem properties;
4. taxonomically divergent communities can be functionally convergent;
5. early arrival can alter dominance and evenness;
6. priority-effect magnitude can depend on species identity, environment or host context;
7. microbiome priority effects can affect host disease.

Core examples include Fukami et al. (2010), Dickie et al. (2012), Fukami (2015), Leopold
& Busby (2020), Weidlich et al. (2021), Debray et al. (2022), and Alonso-Crespo et al.
(2022).

## Strongest novelty that survives the full audit

The strongest contribution is **not** the retained-history statistic itself.

It is the empirical decomposition of *where a historical signal is stored in the present*.

### 1. Target-specific storage on matched experimental units

In two independent randomized systems, the same manipulated history is strongly visible
in community composition and weak / near-null in another present-state target measured on
the same units.

This is not claimed as a universal attenuation law.

### 2. Identity stripping while holding abundances and dimensionality fixed

v31 keeps:

- the exact same abundance values;
- the exact same response dimensionality;
- the same experimental units;
- the same distance;
- the same history model;

and removes only ecological identity labels by sorting each composition vector.

The two systems respond differently:

- microbiome history survives almost completely in unlabeled abundance architecture;
- grassland history loses most of its signal without functional-group identity.

The literature search to date found many studies of dominance, evenness, compositional
contingency and composition–function decoupling, but did **not** identify a previous
priority-effects study using this exact identity-stripping counterfactual.

This is the manuscript's clearest novelty-bearing test.

### 3. Prospective falsification is part of the result

v32 generated an attractive role-based explanation.

v33 then enumerated every equally complex placebo mapping and refuted that interpretation.

This is scientifically useful because it demonstrates that the storage result does not
depend on preserving a convenient post hoc mechanism.

### 4. A narrower microbiome legacy is then prospectively localized

v34-v37 establish that:

- Alternaria has the largest history-level leverage on identity-free architecture;
- the leverage is dominance-centered under both deletion and direct-history designs;
- the dominance shift is positive in 11/12 host genotypes;
- ~79.8% of observed binary-history SS is captured by the genotype-shared component.

v38 then prevents overinterpretation: the archive cannot identify the interaction mechanism.

## One-sentence pitch

> **Assembly history is not stored in one universal feature of a community: two
> independent arrival-order experiments retain history in different structural
> coordinates of the present, and selectively removing ecological identity shows that
> history can reside in abundance architecture itself or chiefly in identity assignment.**

## Why the paper is more than a reanalysis

The source experiments have already been published, so the paper must openly acknowledge
that the biological manipulations are not new.

The new contribution comes from asking a different cross-system question and subjecting
it to prospective derived tests:

- common conceptual estimand across targets;
- matched-unit comparisons;
- identity stripping at fixed dimensionality;
- prospective refutation of universal identity storage;
- complete placebo-mapping falsification of an initially attractive mechanism;
- prospective localization of the strongest microbiome legacy.

This is an analysis paper about a new ecological object—**the structural location of
historical contingency in the present state**—rather than a republication of the original
priority-effect findings.

## Journal strategy

### 1. Ecology Letters — reach submission

**Recommendation: submit here first if maximizing journal ceiling is the priority.**

Current scope emphasizes:

- highly novel ecological research;
- clearly stated hypotheses;
- broad general interest;
- contributions to new ecological ideas rather than descriptive confirmation.

Current Letter limit: **5,000 main-text words** and up to **6 figures/tables/text boxes**.

Why the manuscript can fit:

- it asks a broad community-ecology question rather than a system-specific one;
- two independent manipulated systems support the core phenomenon;
- v31 is a clean prospective counterfactual test;
- v33 is a prospective falsification rather than post hoc storytelling;
- the manuscript is already within the word limit.

Primary desk-reject risks:

- both empirical systems are reanalyses of published experiments;
- only two independent systems support the cross-system storage claim;
- the causal mechanism behind the storage-channel difference is unresolved;
- the small grassland primary panel (n=19) may be viewed as limited for a general claim.

How to pitch it:

Do **not** lead with the E statistic or with “we reanalysed two datasets.”

Lead with:

> Ecology has treated historical contingency mainly as an effect on endpoints. We ask a
> different question: which structural component of the present still contains the
> experimental history?

Then make v31 the conceptual centerpiece.

### 2. Ecology — strongest fit / high-probability target

**Recommendation: best overall fit if acceptance probability and scientific fit are
weighted more heavily than journal ceiling.**

Current scope explicitly welcomes:

- new ecological concepts;
- tests of ecological theory;
- comparative and analytical approaches;
- reanalysis and reinterpretation of published data when the result is original.

Why it fits especially well:

- the paper documents an important ecological phenomenon rather than a new method;
- the two-system design supports generalization without requiring a universal law;
- the prospective negative tests strengthen inference;
- the mechanistic ceiling is honestly defined.

This is the most natural destination if Ecology Letters rejects at editorial screening.

### 3. Oikos — strong conceptual/community-ecology fit

Oikos currently emphasizes innovative empirical and theoretical work on ecological
mechanisms, processes and patterns, and explicitly welcomes provocative ideas and new ways
of interpreting existing data.

Strengths for Oikos:

- community assembly is central;
- the storage-channel framing is conceptually distinctive;
- the paper includes positive and negative prospective tests;
- system-specific storage channels fit the journal's interest in ecological process and
  pattern.

Use as a strong third target or as first choice if the desired paper is more conceptual
and less “urgent-general” than Ecology Letters requires.

### Nature Ecology & Evolution — do not submit in current form

The topic is in scope, but the current evidence base is probably below the journal's
required significance/generalization threshold.

Main limitations:

- two reanalysed systems;
- no new process-level experimental data;
- mechanistic ceiling reached rather than resolved;
- no broad multi-system comparative test.

A Nature Ecology & Evolution attempt would make more sense only after an independent
multi-system validation or a new discriminating mechanism experiment.

## What reviewers are most likely to challenge

### “This is just composition versus function.”

Response:

No. v31 compares two composition targets with identical abundance values and dimensionality
and removes only identity labels. The cross-system difference survives that matched
comparison.

### “The E metric is arbitrary.”

Response:

The biological conclusion does not depend on E alone. Raw nested partial R² is reported,
the null calibration is design-respecting, and the identity-stripping comparisons hold
the response geometry fixed within system.

### “The two systems are too different.”

Response:

That is a limitation for mechanism generality but a strength for demonstrating that
storage is not tied to one taxon or ecosystem. The manuscript should not claim a universal
classification; it claims existence of contrasting storage channels.

### “The Alternaria result is post hoc.”

Response:

The initial Alternaria clue was post hoc and is labeled as such. The claims enter only
through prospectively frozen v34-v37 tests.

### “Why not infer mechanism from TP2?”

Response:

The source analysis explicitly states that TP2 was collected during rust sampling and was
overwhelmed by rust reads. v38 therefore precludes that analysis.
