# BAM inverse-identifiability — figure captions v2

## Figure 1. Forward BAM versus inverse BAM

**Forward BAM generates distributions; inverse BAM returns evidence-compatible worlds.**
(A) In the forward BAM formulation, abiotic support A, biotic permissibility B and movement accessibility M combine to produce the realised distribution G. (B) The inverse problem starts from observed G and can retain multiple BAM worlds because different A/B/M decompositions can map to the same realised intersection. (C) Additional valid evidence contracts or preserves the survivor fiber: complete positives define S0, complete perfect presence/absence defines S1, and temporal/direct A/B/M evidence can contract the fiber further. A singleton is justified only when the relevant evidence fiber is actually unique.

## Figure 2. Positive-superset ceiling and the restrictiveness–ambiguity relation

**Stronger ecological restriction can increase positive-only mechanism ambiguity.**
Each point is one frozen stochastic truth scenario, plotted by complete positive support size and the number of compatible candidate worlds at horizon 40. The jointly constrained A+B+M truth has the smallest support (33 nodes) and the largest compatible set (32 worlds), whereas distance- and barrier-limited truths have support size 96 and only four compatible worlds. The exact ordering is set-theoretic rather than correlational: across all 304 strict support-inclusion pairs, narrower supports retained equal-or-larger compatible-world sets, with zero violations. Point cardinality is descriptive; support inclusion is the exact criterion.

## Figure 3. Progressive evidence contracts BAM survivor fibers

**Complete occurrence does not imply complete mechanism information.**
Across 12 activation-qualified deterministic BAM systems and 768 truth cases, no BAM state was uniquely identified from complete positive occurrences (E0). Unique identification increased to 25/768 with complete realised presence/absence (E1), 159/768 after occupied-node arrival timing (E2), 275/768 after direct abiotic state (E3), 565/768 after direct biotic state (E4), and 768/768 after direct movement-accessibility evidence (E5); complete first-arrival state (E6) retained 768/768. The preregistered claim that movement would always be the final identification bottleneck was refuted because at least one system reached complete state identification before E5.

## Figure 4. Independent stochastic BAM challenge

**The inverse limits persist under an independently implemented stochastic generator.**
Bars show the median number of compatible worlds at horizon 40 across six frozen truth scenarios; annotations give the complete structural support size of each truth. Across 384 eligible runs, truth-retention failures, EOG/evaluator parity mismatches and positive-superset ceiling violations were all zero. Two stronger preregistered expectations were refuted: every structural difference did not necessarily realise a positive witness, and unordered accumulated occurrence history never produced a strict discrimination gain over the final snapshot in the frozen experiment. Unique truth recovery remained zero in every scenario.

## Figure 5. Time-stamped occurrence recovers information discarded by unordered history

**Temporal order is a different evidence class, not simply more occurrences.**
Bars compare median compatible-world counts at horizon 40 using unordered static positive support versus first-occurrence-time compatibility. Static support produced eight equivalence classes; temporal signatures produced 20. Of 48 static M-equivalent pairs, 19 were split by temporal signatures, and temporal evidence strictly contracted the compatible set in 256/384 stochastic runs. Nevertheless, no frozen scenario achieved unique truth recovery, showing that temporal evidence is informative but not sufficient for complete inverse identification.

## Figure 6. Residual BAM ambiguity can be converted into diagnostic measurement design

**Exact finite hitting-set analysis identifies small programme-specific measurement sets.**
Bars show the maximum exact number of targeted direct measurements required across 12 deterministic systems and 768 truth cases to reproduce the relevant target fiber: one A measurement, two B measurements, three jointly optimized A+B measurements and two M-accessibility measurements. The exact audit had zero failures. These numbers are existence bounds inside the frozen finite programme and are not universal ecological constants or claims about field feasibility.
