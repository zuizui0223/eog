# Oikos cover letter draft — NEON metacommunity redundancy

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Species pooling does not create emergent spatial continuity in NEON small-mammal metacommunities,”** for publication in Oikos.

Metacommunity and insurance frameworks motivate a simple expectation: species turnover may allow an aggregate community to remain spatially connected even when no single species spans the same spatial structure. We tested that expectation prospectively across 16 fresh NEON small-mammal sites using fixed trap networks and a preregistered set of spatial worlds.

The expected complementarity did not occur. At all 16 sites, at least one individual species matched the pooled community's maximum world survival; no site contained a world in which the pooled guild survived while every individual species failed. Cross-species-only local rescue was rare, and at ORNL pooling actually reduced spatial world survival by adding unsupported peripheral occurrences.

We therefore report a positive ecological pattern of **spatial redundancy across species**, together with a weakest-link counterexample showing that biodiversity aggregation need not monotonically increase spatial continuity. The result distinguishes topological continuity from the compensatory mechanisms usually invoked for temporal or functional insurance.

The 16-site roster and analysis rules were fixed before response opening, all 16 sites completed the once-only programme, and the confirmatory claim boundary is frozen in the accompanying repository. No post-response site replacement, threshold retuning, or alternative primary scoring rule is used.

We believe the manuscript fits Oikos because it tests a general metacommunity hypothesis with a continental-scale animal dataset and clarifies when aggregation across species is redundant, complementary, or potentially detrimental for a spatial ecological property.
