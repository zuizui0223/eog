# Literature positioning — small-mammal continuity paper

## Ecological center

The paper is not a new metacommunity theory. It asks whether a familiar ecological
mechanism—species turnover and complementarity across heterogeneous space—creates a
specific aggregate property: spatial continuity of observed occurrences.

## Prior theory

Metacommunity theory treats local communities as linked by dispersal and emphasizes the
joint roles of local environmental conditions, biotic interactions and regional movement.

Spatial-insurance theory predicts that spatial or compositional asynchrony can stabilize
aggregate biomass or ecosystem functioning when different species or local communities
respond differently across heterogeneous environments.

Related work has partitioned species insurance, spatial insurance and cross-community
asynchrony, showing that aggregate stability can emerge from compensation among
populations, species and local communities.

These are prior art. We do not claim novelty for species turnover, spatial insurance,
metacommunity connectivity in the general sense, or assemblage permutation nulls.

## Distinct question

The present endpoint is not biomass stability, temporal invariability or ecosystem
function.

It asks:

> Does pooling species create topological spatial continuity that no individual species
> possesses?

The prospective answer was no across all 16 fresh NEON sites.

## Ecological interpretation

The result is best described as occupancy-continuity redundancy.

At every site, at least one individual species matched or exceeded the pooled community's
surviving-world fraction. Thirteen of 16 sites contained multiple independently continuous
species, and 32 species filled this role across the fresh denominator. No strict emergent
world occurred and local cross-species rescue was rare.

This resembles the broader ecological distinction between complementarity and dominance
or redundancy, but the measured property is occurrence connectivity rather than
productivity or stability.

## Relation to spatial insurance

The result should be presented as a boundary on transfer of the insurance intuition:

- compensation can stabilize aggregate function;
- species turnover can maintain different taxa across heterogeneous locations;
- but those facts do not imply that the union of taxa has greater all-occurrence spatial
  continuity than its best component species.

This is compatible with prior studies showing that aggregate metacommunity properties can
sometimes be driven by a few common species rather than spatial insurance.

## Strong but safe novelty statement

> We prospectively tested whether species turnover generates an emergent spatial
> continuity property in real small-mammal metacommunities and found the opposite
> organization: continuity was occupancy-continuity redundant at every site, with no spatial world
> requiring cross-species complementarity for survival.

## Do not claim

- first test of metacommunity turnover;
- first evidence for species redundancy;
- first spatial-insurance test;
- proof that turnover never matters for connectivity;
- proof that dominant species cause the pattern;
- general failure of biodiversity insurance.

## Core references

- Leibold et al. and later metacommunity syntheses for local-regional community dynamics.
- Thompson et al. 2020 for a process-based metacommunity framework linking local and
  regional processes.
- Lamy et al. 2019, Ecology, for species versus spatial insurance in a natural
  metacommunity.
- Hammond et al. 2020, Ecosphere, for partitioning local, metapopulation and
  cross-community sources of stabilization.
- Spatial-insurance literature following Loreau et al. 2003 for the compensation
  hypothesis being tested by analogy.
