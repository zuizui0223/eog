# Journal fit — NEON small-mammal continuity

Checked: 2026-09-25

## Primary: Oikos

Oikos explicitly publishes innovative empirical and theoretical ecology spanning taxa and
disciplines, with emphasis on ecological mechanisms, processes and patterns.

This paper tests a mechanistic metacommunity hypothesis prospectively across a
continental-scale animal dataset and returns a clear negative-to-positive reframing:
spatial continuity is occupancy-continuity redundant rather than turnover-generated.

Oikos is the strongest first route because:
- the question is metacommunity mechanism rather than method development;
- the result speaks to turnover, redundancy and aggregation across levels of organization;
- recent Oikos papers directly address metacommunity sorting and spatial turnover.

## Secondary: Journal of Animal Ecology

Journal of Animal Ecology seeks animal-ecology studies that test and advance broad
ecological principles and welcomes large-scale open-data work.

Strength:
- 16 independent small-mammal sites;
- prospective ecological hypothesis;
- broad redundancy-versus-complementarity principle.

Desk risk:
- the one-step connectivity endpoint is method-specific and must be explained as an
  ecological property rather than an EOG technical result.

## Fallback: Ecography

Ecography is strong for spatial community ecology, distributional structure and
metacommunity networks. It is a natural fallback if the paper is framed more strongly
around spatial occurrence structure and less around biodiversity-insurance theory.

## Submission order

1. Oikos
2. Journal of Animal Ecology
3. Ecography

## Cover-letter center

Do not lead with EOG.

Lead with:
- a preregistered metacommunity complementarity hypothesis;
- 16 fresh NEON small-mammal systems;
- complete absence of emergent guild connectivity;
- low cross-species rescue;
- the ORNL weakest-link counterexample.

EOG should appear in Methods as the finite-world spatial measurement framework.
