# Publication separation from EOG-WF v1

## Purpose

This paper must remain scientifically distinct from the existing EOG-WF Methods in Ecology and Evolution manuscript.

## EOG-WF owns

- Layer A world contraction / falsification;
- Layer B predictive complementarity;
- the favorable / favorable / adverse three-endpoint pattern;
- endpoint-specific log-loss values, placebos and cross-system predictive interpretation;
- the conclusion that Layer-B added value is context dependent.

## Inferential-accessibility paper owns

- the complete 31-STOP denominator as a data-to-endpoint accessibility audit;
- classification of blockers by transport, registry/geometry, separation/linkage, semantic and estimability layers;
- endpoint conversion count only (3/34), not comparative endpoint performance;
- cross-program integration with 284b Level C;
- negative-state identifiability as a later layer of accessibility;
- the proposed inferential-accessibility manifest.

## Reuse ceiling

The three EOG scored endpoints may appear only as the count of systems that reached a valid scored endpoint. Their favorable/adverse effect sizes are unnecessary for the new paper and should not be reproduced in Results except where required to explain that endpoint accessibility is outcome-direction neutral.

## No duplicate claim

EOG-WF asks: does the structural representation add predictive value, and when?

The new paper asks: what information layers must a public ecological system expose before that or any similarly frozen endpoint can be evaluated without post-inspection repair?

These are distinct estimands and conclusions.

## 284b separation

The 284b M1+M2 Ecology Letters manuscript owns the formal unresolved-state identities and positive Level-A held-out closure.

The inferential-accessibility paper may cite the Level-C qualification ledger only as evidence that data availability can fail at negative-state identification after relation/event/function architecture exists. It should not reproduce the M1 algebra or the Level-A empirical result as a focal contribution.
