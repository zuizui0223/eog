# Open data are not necessarily inferentially open: a prospective audit of ecological endpoint accessibility

Working target: Ecological Informatics
Status: manuscript spine v1
Evidence: existing terminal ledgers only; no new candidate or biological-response opening

## One-sentence result

> Across prospectively screened public ecological systems, the dominant bottleneck was often not model performance but whether source transport, registries, geometry, response semantics and negative biological states were sufficiently reproducible to define the intended endpoint at all.

## Why this is not a failure paper

The paper measures a hidden denominator: how often a public ecological system can be converted into a prospectively specified inferential endpoint without repairing the source or redefining the target after inspection.

Two programmes expose different layers:

1. EOG asks whether public monitoring systems can reach a valid held-out predictive endpoint under response-blind source, geometry, effort and semantic gates.
2. 284b Level C asks a deeper question after relation/event/function channels exist: whether the negative biological state required to falsify a hard dependency is itself observable under a calibrated process.

Together they separate data availability from inferential accessibility.

## 1. Inferential-accessibility stack

publicly discoverable
→ transportable and version-identifiable
→ sampling registry and geometry reproducible
→ response physically and semantically separable
→ effort/calendar/linkage identifiable
→ endpoint estimable
→ negative state identifiable when falsification requires it
→ scored ecological inference

Open data concern availability of bytes or records. Inferentially open data additionally expose the identities, geometry, observation semantics and state calibration required by a declared ecological claim without post-inspection repair.

This is endpoint-relative, not a quality score for a repository.

## 2. EOG prospective endpoint funnel

Frozen scientific denominator:

- scored predictive endpoints: 3
- scientific/protocol STOPs: 31
- scientific denominator: 34
- scored conversion: 3/34 = 8.82%
- administrative exclusions outside denominator: 3

The three scored systems include two favorable endpoints and one adverse endpoint, so endpoint accessibility was not conditioned on getting a favorable answer. Candidate hunting was hard-stopped after endpoint 3.

## 3. A deeper bottleneck: 284b Level C

A finite Level-C architecture screen evaluated 12 candidates. Two of 12 reached architecture qualification.

The retained systems were:

- Cremastra appendiculata var. variabilis: independent breeding-system relation evidence, same-key fruit outcome and pollinia-carrying camera function stream.
- Belonocnema treatae / live-oak budbreak: independent host-specificity evidence, natural adult-emergence event stream and separate budbreak function stream.

Both passed materializability of relation, event and positive function channels.

Both stopped because functional absence was not independently calibrated.

For Cremastra, camera non-detection could not be converted into absence of effective pollination without known detection sensitivity and false-negative calibration.

For Belonocnema, missing or unobserved budbreak could not be prospectively separated from true absence of usable new tissue at the required key.

Hard endpoint opening: 0/2.
Biological negatives declared: 0/2.

A dedicated candidate-specific calibration audit passed 0/2 systems. A further raw/supplementary reconstruction audit again passed 0/2. The terminal requirement was new calibration measurement, not reinterpretation of existing zeros.

## 4. Unified result

The combined evidence supports a layered model:

1. Transport openness — can the exact source/version be retrieved reproducibly?
2. Registry openness — can the scientific sampling universe be reconstructed response-independently?
3. Relational openness — can geometry, time, effort and identifiers link the streams?
4. Semantic openness — can missing values, failures and surveyed negatives be distinguished?
5. State openness — is the state required to contradict the claim, especially absence, independently identifiable?
6. Inferential openness — can the intended endpoint finally be evaluated without post-outcome repair?

EOG supplies a denominator through the earlier layers. Level C demonstrates that even after those layers are largely satisfied, negative-state identifiability can remain the binding constraint.

## 5. Proposed inferential-accessibility manifest

Datasets intended for reusable ecological inference should, where relevant, expose:

1. immutable source/version identity;
2. physical entity checksums;
3. sampling-unit registry;
4. site/geometry registry;
5. survey or effort calendar;
6. response-to-unit linkage keys;
7. explicit missingness states;
8. surveyed-negative definition;
9. observation-failure states;
10. detection/absence calibration when hard negative inference is intended.

This does not require every dataset to support every inference. It declares which inferential operations the released data can support.

## 6. Limits

The EOG candidate set is not a random sample of all ecological datasets. Therefore 8.82% is not a global estimate of endpoint readiness.

The STOP rate is conditional on demanding, prospectively frozen predictive endpoints.

The Level-C systems are two architecture-qualified examples and do not estimate how common absence-calibration problems are across ecology.

The defensible general result is structural: public availability and valid inference are separated by multiple measurable information layers.

## Conclusion

> Data openness is not binary once the target is ecological inference. Public records become inferentially open only when source identity, sampling geometry, linkage, observation semantics and the states required by the intended claim are reproducible without post-outcome repair.

## Claim boundary

Do not claim that 8.82% is globally representative, that STOP systems are low-quality or non-FAIR, that a STOP is a negative ecological result, or that either Level-C system biologically violated its dependency.
