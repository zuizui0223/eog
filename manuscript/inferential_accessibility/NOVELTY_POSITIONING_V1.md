# Novelty positioning v1

## Nearest neighboring literature

### FAIR implementation for ecological data

Jantzen & Vriend (2026, Ecological Informatics 95:103712) translate FAIR principles into practical ecological-data components and workflows centered on metadata, storage, standards, structure and long-term reuse.

That work asks how data providers/users can make ecological datasets more reusable.

Our paper asks a downstream question:

> Given a publicly available ecological system and a prospectively declared scientific endpoint, can the available source actually instantiate every object required to evaluate that endpoint without post-inspection repair?

The EOG denominator is therefore not a FAIR score. A source can be findable, accessible and reusable for many purposes while still lacking the exact registry, linkage, effort semantics or negative-state calibration required by one endpoint.

### LIES observation-process framework

Chadwick et al. (2024, Trends in Ecology & Evolution 39:368–380) organize ecological observation problems around Latency, Identifiability, Effort and Scale and connect observation-process complexity to inferential solutions.

Our manuscript is complementary rather than competitive.

LIES provides a conceptual typology of observation-process problems. The present paper contributes:

1. a prospective operational denominator across real public ecological systems;
2. terminal evidence for where source-to-endpoint conversion stops before modeling;
3. explicit separation between endpoint unavailability and adverse biological evidence;
4. an additional negative-state-identifiability layer demonstrated after relation/event/function channels already exist;
5. an endpoint-relative inferential-accessibility manifest.

## Novelty sentence

> We move from asking whether ecological data are reusable or whether observation processes are complex to measuring, prospectively and with a retained denominator, whether public data expose the exact information states needed to evaluate a declared ecological endpoint.

## What not to claim

- Do not claim FAIR is insufficient in general; inferential accessibility is endpoint-relative and complementary to FAIR.
- Do not claim LIES omits identifiability; identifiability is explicitly central to LIES.
- Do not present the STOP taxonomy as a replacement for observation-process modeling.
- Do not conflate repository quality with endpoint readiness.

## Journal fit

Ecological Informatics is a plausible primary target because the contribution sits at the interface of ecological data infrastructure, reproducible computational workflows, metadata semantics and downstream inference.

The paper should be framed as an empirical methodological audit with a reusable manifest, not as a Perspective based only on opinion.
